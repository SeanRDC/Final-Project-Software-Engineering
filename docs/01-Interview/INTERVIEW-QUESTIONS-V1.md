# 🗣️ Open-Ended Interview Questions

This document outlines structured interview questions for gathering insights about clinic workflows, pain points, and requirements for a digital patient management system.

---

## 1. Understanding Their Daily Headaches (Project Background & Site Context)

- **1.1** How does a typical busy day look in the clinic, like during peak weeks?  
  What’s the most stressful part of dealing with all the records right now?

- **1.2** If you could magically make one time-consuming task disappear from your daily routine, what would it be?

- **1.3** Where do you usually sit or stand when you're writing down patient info?  
  Are you always at a desk, or do you move around the clinic?  
  *Follow-up:* Are there times where you multitask with writing down patient info along with other duties?  
  Does this affect your performance and concentration for said duties?

- **1.4** Has the clinic ever tried using any third-party clinic software or commercial apps in the past?  
  What made them difficult to use, too complicated, or just not a good fit for how our university clinic operates?

---

## 2. Figuring Out Who Does What (Use-Case Diagrams & Actors)

- **2.1** Considering a digital system for managing patients and appointments, who do you think will use this and benefit from it the most?  
  Will it just be nurses and doctors, or do student assistants and admin staff need to use it too?

- **2.2** When it comes to managing the appointments and the daily queue, who should have the final say?  
  For example, should any staff member be able to cancel or override an appointment, or should that be restricted to specific people?

- **2.3** Would you want students to be able to book their own appointments from their phones, or is it better for your workflow if they just walk in and you manage the schedule at the front desk?

- **2.4** Can you walk me through a typical day-to-day operation in the clinic under the current system?

---

## 3. Following the Patient's Journey (Level 0 & 1 Data Flow Diagrams)

- **3.1** Let’s imagine a student walks in with a bad fever.  
  From the second they say "hello" to the moment they leave, what exactly gets written down, and who writes it?

- **3.2** When the clinic is full and people need to wait, how do you decide who goes next?  
  Do they just go in order, or do you bump emergencies to the front?  
  How can the system help you manage that line?

- **3.3** Let's say the waiting area is busy.  
  A walk-in student has been waiting for 15 minutes, but another student just walked in exactly on time for their scheduled appointment.  
  How should the system prioritize them? Who gets called next?  
  *Follow-up:* In the case where one gets picked over the other, how do you accommodate the person not chosen?

---

## 4. Mapping Out the Screens and Database (Design Layout & Functional Requirements)

- **4.1** When you pull up a student's record, what are the top three things you need to see instantly to do your job?

- **4.2** Can you show me the physical forms you use the most every day?  
  Which parts of these forms are annoying to fill out or often left blank?

- **4.3** Do you want the system to keep track of how many medicines (like paracetamol) you give out and warn you when you're running low?

- **4.4** At the end of the semester, what kind of summary reports does the university administration ask you to submit?

---

## 5. Keeping Data Safe and Reliable (Data Privacy Act & Implementation)

- **5.1** Since student health records are highly sensitive, how do you currently make sure they stay private?  
  How should we handle student consent when moving this online?

- **5.2** Have you ever had the internet drop in the clinic?  
  If the Wi-Fi goes down for an hour, how would you prefer to handle walk-ins until it comes back online?

- **5.3** If you had to train a new nurse on this system, what features would make it easiest for them to learn quickly?

---

## 6. Working Together in Quick Cycles (Agile Development)

- **6.1** Since we work in short bursts and want to give you something useful as quickly as possible, what is the absolute most urgent problem we should solve in our first two days?

- **6.2** Because this approach relies heavily on your feedback, we'd love to have a quick, 10-to-15-minute check-in on Thursday.  
  Who would be the best person in the clinic to be our main point of contact for these quick updates?

- **6.3** When we bring in an early, working piece of the system like just the digital queueing screen, would you prefer we sit down together so you can click through it while we watch?
